ABDM's [Health Data Management Policy, clause 16.4 and introductory scope](sources.md#identity-and-privacy) supports voluntary digital-health participation and non-denial for lack of the health ID. This does not make every identifier optional for every insurer or regulated transaction; maintain the care workflow separately from those later requirements.

## Purpose-specific processing and disclosure

IN-ID-06: Model processing authority by purpose and applicable policy, with notice/version, scope, actor, relationship, evidence, recorded/effective/expiry times and revocation. Do not force all processing into consent if another reviewed duty applies, and do not invent a legal basis such as a foreign jurisdiction's terminology. General hospital terms, treatment consent, procedure consent, ABDM disclosure and marketing permission are different records.

IN-ID-07: Record sensitive reads, searches, disclosures, proxy grants, merges and exports. A consent receipt is not by itself an object-access grant. Withdrawal prevents future use where applicable, but does not silently erase a signed clinical record, statutory retention obligation or legal hold. Hospital privacy/legal reviewers must define those interactions, children/guardian handling and grievance procedures.

The DPDP Rules 2025 have phased commencement: rule 1 brings rules 1, 2 and 17–21 into force on publication, rule 4 after one year, and rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 after eighteen months. Verify the Act's separate commencement notification and any corrigenda/amendments for the deployment date. Do not describe all obligations as already commenced merely because a summary announces notification. See [MeitY primary text and review limits](sources.md#identity-and-privacy).

This specification does not decide Aadhaar authentication authority, a universal retention period, DPDP exemptions or data-export/residency permissions. Store reviewed policy applicability, and have privacy/legal owners approve it before activation. Security and isolation are Helivanta requirements irrespective of a future statutory phase.

## ABDM and clinical exchange boundary

IN-ID-08: ABHA linking, facility/practitioner registration, consented exchange and portal authorization remain separate operations. Track verified linking state and external transaction identity; a successful link does not expose all hospitals' records. HIP/HIU integration must follow the selected environment's enrollment, credentials, consent and data-transfer contracts, with idempotent callbacks and explicit failed/pending reconciliation.

IN-ID-09: At research time, NRCeS identifies **6.5.0** as the current published FHIR R4 guide while **7.0.0** is served from a preview location. Select and pin the integration's agreed published package/version and terminology; validate its required elements and document bundle references. A generic FHIR resource or valid JSON is not ABDM conformance. No adapter is implemented by these docs. [Primary guide](sources.md#exchange-and-revenue).

Authorized sharing needs current source-hospital permission, recipient scope and applicable exchange evidence. Minimize clinical payloads in callbacks/events; use protected owner reads or approved encrypted exchange. Out-of-order/replayed callbacks cannot restore a revoked grant or attach another patient's document.

Review with registration operations, clinical governance, patient identity, privacy/security and the actual exchange consumer. Test wrong-hospital linking, expired/revoked proxy, withdrawn disclosure, duplicate/mismatched callbacks, missing mandatory profile fields, incorrect patient references and external-service outages. Never test against real identities or submit live national-ID transactions without explicit authorization.
