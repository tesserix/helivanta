# Home page care board

Resolves #1354. Follows #1087 (public landing at `/`) and the organization model in [docs/domain/organization](../../domain/organization/README.md).

## Why

The current home page lists modules but never shows what Helivanta does for a hospital. A hospital owner, Medical Superintendent or state buyer should understand within one screen that management, administration and departments hand work to each other through one system. They should then be able to follow a patient through the hospital, see how medicines and money stay accountable, see how AI will help without taking decisions away from people, and find each product and the security behind it without leaving the page.

Helivanta is built for hospitals in any country and launches in India first. Copy is global; India appears as the first country, never as the product's only setting.

## What the page says

1. **Hero.** One headline and a short paragraph: every department and every level of management working from the same records, built for hospitals everywhere and launching first in India.
2. **Care board.** An animated diagram. Management (governing body, hospital leadership, department heads) on the left, departments and services (wards and OPD, pharmacy, laboratory, central store, maintenance, emergency) on the right, administration (HR and staff, finance, purchase) underneath, Helivanta in the middle. Every exchange travels through the middle, so departments share records instead of re-typing them.
3. **Stories.** Five selectable stories play on the board, request by request: a ward runs short of a medicine, a purchase needs sign-off, an emergency patient arrives, a nurse moves to the ICU, management sees the whole hospital. A numbered list narrates each request and names the sending and receiving teams.
4. **Patient journey.** One patient moves along a corridor of eight stations: registration, consultation, lab order, result, prescription, dispensing, billing, discharge and follow-up. Each station adds an entry to one shared record shown beneath the corridor, owned by the team that wrote it ([clinical operations](../../domain/india/clinical-operations.md) IN-CL-01, IN-CL-05 to IN-CL-07).
5. **Pharmacy and finance trail.** A ledger with two lanes, stock and money, feeding one append-only trail: goods received with batch and expiry, quarantine release, dispensing against a prescription, supplier invoice as a payable, settlement, a count correction that adds a reversing entry and keeps the original, and an auditor who can read the trail but not change it ([pharmacy and supply](../../domain/india/pharmacy-and-supply.md) IN-PH-03, IN-PH-04, IN-PH-08; [revenue](../../domain/india/billing-and-insurance.md) IN-RC-02, IN-RC-11).
6. **Pharmacy stock that checks itself.** A shelf of six medicines at one pharmacy outlet, each with what can be dispensed, its reorder level and its earliest expiry. An automatic check sweeps the shelf, marks each medicine in stock, running low, out of stock, expiring within 30 days or in quarantine, and counts each kind. Each finding then follows its own route: medicines to order go to the stock forecasting agent, which drafts one purchase request for the pharmacy manager to approve; a short-dated batch gets a use-first and transfer suggestion that the store in-charge approves; a delivery in quarantine goes straight to a pharmacist with no agent involved. Stock positions with dispensable units, quarantine and expiry bands exist today (#1226); reorder levels (#524), the automatic check, demand forecasting (#742), expiry-risk suggestions (#747) and release from quarantine are planned and labelled so.
7. **Maintenance and compliance that check themselves.** One site's maintenance register of eight electrical, structural, equipment, fire safety, lift and medical gas items, each with its check, due date or certificate expiry and last result. An automatic check marks each item current, due soon (within 14 days), overdue, certificate expiring (within 60 days) or failed check, and counts each kind. Overdue and due work goes to a maintenance planning agent that drafts work orders for the engineering manager; an expiring certificate goes to a compliance agent that drafts the renewal pack for the compliance manager; a failed safety check goes straight to a biomedical engineer with no agent, and the equipment stays out of service until it passes a retest. Everything on this board is planned in FacilityConnect and labelled so: inspections (#300, #308, #629, #634), certificates (#278, #301), equipment maintenance and status (#639, #640), the obligations library (#642), work-order priorities (#648), and the agents (#1357, #748). The copy says which checks apply depends on the country, state and facility type, and that Helivanta keeps the evidence but does not certify compliance.
8. **AI with a person in charge.** A supervisor agent in the middle routes hospital events to specialist agents (clinical notes, coding, stock forecasting, claims, rosters). Each agent drafts; a named person reviews, edits and approves; the approved action is recorded with its AI-assisted provenance. Five selectable scenarios: discharge summary, medicine reorder, insurance claim, shift cover, pharmacy audit. Drawn from [AI enablement](../../ai-enablement.md) (#717–#753); everything in this section is labelled Planned.
9. **Why Helivanta.** Four reasons drawn from the [organization model](../../domain/organization/README.md): one owner per record, access that follows the job, the hospital's own workflows on top, and one product for every kind of hospital. Each states what is available today and what is planned.
10. **Government and private.** The two hierarchies side by side. The government example is India's, the first market, from [public and private sectors](../../domain/india/public-and-private-sectors.md); the copy says other countries map their own levels onto the same model.
11. **Platform.** The catalogue's four groups (patient care; diagnostics, medicines and supplies; hospital operations; relationships and administration), each listing its modules with their statuses.
12. **Trust.** The certification placeholders, each labelled "Not yet certified".
13. **Reviews and closing call to action.** Unchanged in substance: placeholders are labelled as such. "Start your workspace" opens `/workspace`, which keeps the previous landing page for signed-in work.

## Decisions

- **D1 Single source for availability.** Every story, station, scenario and product names catalogue modules by their `platform-catalog.ts` name and shows that module's status. The names are a type derived from the catalogue, so a renamed or removed module fails type-check. Each catalogue module belongs to exactly one product in the tour; a test enforces it.
- **D2 Truthful copy.** Boards describe how Helivanta is designed to connect a hospital. Configurable approval chains, posting-driven access, department scopes, hospital billing, signed-prescription integration, the tamper-evident audit archive and all AI assistance are planned and labelled so. Pharmacy batches, quarantine, the stock register and supplier payables and settlements exist today, so the catalogue's pharmacy description is corrected to say so. No certification, endorsement, clinical, regulatory or AI-performance claim.
- **D3 Motion.** Six orchestrated boards (care, patient journey, pharmacy and finance trail, pharmacy stock, maintenance and compliance, AI). Each animates only while at least half of it is in view, so one moves at a time as the reader scrolls. One shared player drives them: hydration-safe start, a step timer, pause, and reduced-motion fallback. The pause control is a small icon-only button with an accessible name ("Pause animation" or "Play animation"), satisfying WCAG 2.2.2 without a text pill. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` every board shows its whole sequence statically with no autoplay and no pause control. No section entrance animates.
- **D4 Accessible content.** Boards are decorative (`aria-hidden`) and described by a caption. Selector buttons (`aria-pressed`) and numbered narration lists (`aria-current="step"`) carry the full content for screen readers, keyboard users and small screens. Boards are shown from the `md` breakpoint; below it the narration alone tells each story.
- **D5 CSP.** The page stays inside the nonce policy (#1113): framer-motion writes only `style` attributes, which `style-src-attr` allows; the typeface is self-hosted through `next/font/local`, served from `'self'`. `e2e/tests/csp.spec.ts` already visits `/`.
- **D6 Visual identity.** A night-shift palette scoped to the home page: deep blue-teal canvas, with three signal colours that encode the kind of exchange (teal for care, amber for medicines and stock, periwinkle for approvals, money and staffing). Typeface: Anek Latin (Ek Type, SIL OFL 1.1), using its width axis for condensed headlines. Tokens are scoped to `.home-night` in `apps/shell/app/globals.css`; the rest of the public site uses the shared theme tokens from #1265.
- **D7 Security copy names only verified controls as current.** This governs the security and documents section, which this slice does not build. Current: tenant and hospital row-level isolation enforced by PostgreSQL, a declared permission on every route checked by OpenFGA that fails closed, 404 for another tenant's records, mutual TLS between services in the cluster, encryption at rest by Google Cloud storage, secrets held in OpenBao, and a nonce-based content security policy. Documents: upload to quarantine, malware scan, release only when clean, permission-checked download; built and tested for staff files and supplier licence evidence but not yet switched on in production, so labelled "Built, not yet enabled". Planned: tamper-evident audit archive. Source: [security and compliance](../../architecture/04-security-and-compliance.md), [file storage and scanning](../../architecture/07-file-storage-and-scanning.md).
- **D8 Stock findings derive from the numbers.** Each medicine's finding is computed from its dispensable and quarantined units, reorder level and earliest expiry with one precedence (out of stock, quarantine, running low, expiring, in stock), and the board's counts and routes are computed from the findings, so the shelf, the counts and the routes cannot disagree. Severity is encoded by fill, border and icon within the three signal colours of D6 rather than a fourth alarm colour. Maintenance findings follow the same rule with one precedence (failed check, overdue, certificate expiring, due soon, current): a failed result outranks every date.

## Not covered

- Separate per-product pages, and a per-product tour with an index that follows the reader's scroll.
- A check that did not run is not shown. The product must report it as missing rather than as zero overdue (#1358).
- Backing each module labelled available with one guarantee a test proves (#1359).
- Dark and light switching for the public site (#1088). The home page is dark by design; signup keeps its light theme.
- The security and documents section. When it is built, its copy follows D7.
- Live data on any board. They illustrate flows; they do not call any API.
- Localised copy for other countries. Country packs are a product concern; the page names India only as the first market.
